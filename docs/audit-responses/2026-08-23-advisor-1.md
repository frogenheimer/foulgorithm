# Foulgorithm response to the Technical Audit & Architectural Blueprint (v2026.08)

**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Responding to:** "Technical Audit & Architectural Blueprint: Foulgorithm", peer review and refinement proposal
**Prepared by:** the Foulgorithm project

Thank you for the audit. It is sharp and it forced two useful pieces of work:
a correction to our own published methodology page and a labelling change we
should have made anyway. This response goes through your three headline
vulnerabilities, the dial matrix and the odds strategy in order, states what we
adopted, and states what we rejected with the evidence for rejecting it.

---

## First, a correction we owe you

Your second headline vulnerability, the latent match correlation deficit at
slope 1.644, was reasoned correctly from a stale number in our methodology
page. We re-measured match-level variance per character on 23 August 2026 and
the result supersedes it:

| character | window | n | slope | model variance | actual variance | fitted shared sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| house (Tayler) | 2024 | 463 | 2.003 | 26.93 | 24.43 | 0 |
| house (Tayler) | 2023 | 875 | 1.589 | 26.71 | 24.06 | 0 |
| Alan | 2024 | 463 | 0.505 | 23.59 | 27.86 | 0.088 |

The decomposition separates E[Var(T|M)] from the shared term and is validated
against planted shared factors of 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 in our test suite, so a
zero means zero rather than a broken measurement.

Three consequences:

1. **The house model has no missing shared variance.** Its predictive variance
   for a total already exceeds what happens by about 10%. A latent match effect
   would widen a distribution that is already too wide.
2. **The house slope of 2.0 is a discrimination fault, not a spread fault.**
   Point estimates barely vary between matches (sd 1.09 against outcomes at
   5.29). A mean-1 random effect does not move a point estimate, so the
   proposed fix does not touch the actual defect.
3. **The original 1.644 was one configuration's number read as the model's.**
   Slope runs 0.44 to 2.00 across characters on identical data. Alan does carry
   about 9% shared variance because his dispersion is pinned at 1.00, which is
   his temperament working as designed rather than a defect.

So your Phase 2 (latent match random effect, bivariate negative binomial) is
retired on measurement. What survives from the same family of concern: a
referee effect estimated alongside team effects rather than as a ratio of
averages, and modelling the total first then allocating it to players. Both
stay on our plan. One caveat we hold against ourselves: our ECE and log-loss
diagnostics currently disagree at player level, that disagreement is entangled
with a live calibration question, and we will re-run the variance study once
that settles.

## 1A. Spatial interaction matrices

**Rejected as specified, with a partial concession.**

The coordinate data the fix requires does not exist for us. Positional and
heatmap data died with the January 2026 Opta termination and nothing free
replaced it. We hold formation slot codes from the league's lineup feed, not
positions on the pitch, and our data budget is fixed at £0.

The r = -0.003 you cite also measured something narrower than "pitch positions
are ignored". It tested whether facing the opponent's best foul-winner predicts
a player's fouls beyond what the opposing club's team-level rate already
implies, over 9,419 player-matches. It does not: the effect is real (predicted
and actual both climb across buckets) and the team-level opponent factor
already carries all of it.

**The concession:** a coarse channel version of your idea is testable with
formation slots alone. Whether a full-back facing a formation with wide wingers
concedes more than one facing a narrow diamond is a geometry question the
-0.003 test never asked. Registered as a future study.

## 1B. Minute-dependent context factors

**Accepted in spirit, folded into a larger workstream.**

You are right that a sub entering at minute 75 lives in a different fouls
regime from a starter at minute 1. Our phase 1 centrepiece is a substitute
programme: a historical substitute dataset (entry minute, position, opponent,
referee, game state at entry approximated from the half-time score), a study of
sub foul rates by entry window and game state, and a model change gated on that
study finding signal. Conditioning context multipliers by branch falls out of
that work naturally.

## 1C. Persona amplification

**Partially right, remedy rejected, alternative adopted.**

Two factual points first. The baseline statistical engine is already objective:
the house parameter set runs amplify = 1.0, so does Tayler, and the calibration
correction is fitted on house output. Amplification exists only in the four
deliberately opinionated characters. And personality already lives partly
downstream as you recommend: each character has its own selection preference
function over the same candidate pool.

The characters are the product. Five parameter sets over one model is what
makes the bake-off fair, and deleting amplification collapses five temperaments
into five house models. We will not do that.

The kernel of your point we accept: the site publishes character probabilities
with the same visual weight as house probabilities, and a reader taking Alan's
65% at face value is consuming a number made overconfident on purpose. The fix
we adopt is labelling rather than deletion: the house probability will be
published beside every character number with the deviation quantified, so a
character's opinion is visibly an opinion about a stated baseline.

## 2. The dial matrix, row by row

**Alan.** Constraining k >= 5 sands the character toward the house model, so we
reject it: his overreaction to 2-match noise is the hypothesis he exists to
test, and the season-long record is the control on it. Your second suggestion
is better than your first: decay by matches played rather than calendar days is
a genuinely testable improvement, and we have registered it as a harness
experiment on the house model. The counter-argument it must beat: regime change
happens in calendar time, and a 6-month absence genuinely stales information.
One walk-forward run decides it.

**Lily.** An age-curve decay factor is registered as a future idea. Low
priority until the characters have a longer graded record to test against.

**Valentina.** Your remediation is already implemented. Head-to-head is shrunk
by split-half reliability: the measured split-half correlation is 0.138, so
about a quarter of any observed pairing residual is believed, and the shipped
effect was measured accuracy-neutral over 26,329 walk-forward player-matches.
This one suggests the audit worked from the methodology page rather than the
code, which is understandable, and we flag it because it validates the
implementation.

**Tayler.** Rejected. k = 30 is his temperament and the point of him: he is the
calibration anchor and is documented as "best calibrated, least useful". The
neutral default the project actually reasons from is the house set at k = 6.
Cutting Tayler to 15 just makes a sixth character between two existing ones.

**Bdog.** Your feedback-loop concern does not yet bite, for a reason worth
stating precisely: his fade is a fixed constant (0.4), not a parameter fitted
against outcomes, so there is no training loop to become circular. We have
recorded a guard so that if the fade is ever fitted, the design moves to
out-of-fold predictions. Benchmarking him against an ensemble median in
reporting is accepted.

## 3. Odds sourcing

**Rejected as specified, replaced with what is actually available.**

On the facts: we verified market coverage on 21 August 2026 and recorded it in
an architecture decision record. The Odds API's full soccer player-prop list
carries no player fouls market and no player tackles market; its soccer props
come from US bookmakers only, stated in their own documentation. We could not
verify the SharpAPI tier as described, and it is moot if the market is not
carried. No archive of UK player-fouls prices exists at any price that we can
find. So the free-tier route you propose has nothing to fetch for our primary
market.

On the policy: intercepting bookmaker internal JSON endpoints breaches their
terms, requires permanent maintenance against anti-bot measures, and creates a
republication problem worse than the collection problem. It also conflicts with
two standing rules of this project: a hard £0 constraint and a stated position
of not using sources whose owners have objected. We note that the other
external audit we received explicitly warns against building core systems on
undocumented scraping endpoints, and on this point we side with it.

One factual softening of your premise: we already ingest closing match odds
(football-data.co.uk, 2000 onward, full coverage from 2019/20), and they drive
a real market baseline for the match-level machinery plus our game-state
studies. The blindness is specific to player props, not total.

**What we will do instead:** a manual price capture page for the specific lines
we track, so closing line value becomes measurable on an honestly small sample;
the US-books card-prop market wired as a labelled weak comparator; and a
quarterly re-survey of licensed fouls-prop availability, because market
coverage changes and our August 2026 answer should not be treated as permanent.

## 4. Disposition of your roadmap

| Your item | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Remove Step 4 amplification from core baseline | Baseline already has amplify = 1.0. Adopted instead: house number published beside every character number |
| Refactor time decay to active matches | Registered as a harness experiment on the house model |
| Spatial channel interaction matrices | Blocked on data. Coarse formation-slot version registered |
| Latent match random effect | Retired by the 23 August measurement |
| Bivariate negative binomial for totals | Retired with it |
| Free-tier odds scraper | Rejected on coverage, terms and policy |
| Log opening, lineup and closing lines | Adopted in manual form for tracked lines |
| Pivot evaluation to EV versus closing odds | Adopted only to the extent prices exist: cards comparator and manual sample. Calibration stays the primary measure because it needs no price |

## What we took from your audit

Three things, concretely: the labelling fix for character probabilities, the
matches-based decay experiment, and the framing of minute-dependent context
that is now folded into the substitute programme. The audit also indirectly
caused us to correct a stale claim on our own methodology page, which is worth
more than it sounds for a project whose only real asset is being trustworthy
about itself.
