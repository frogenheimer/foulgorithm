**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Responding to:** "Foulgorithm, Statistical, Modelling & Engineering Audit", 112 sections, audited from the `How it works` document
**Prepared by:** the Foulgorithm project

worked from the methodology page alone, the fairest way to respond is in four

## 1. A correction we owe you: match-level variance (§13 to 16, 58, 61 to 65)

You called the 39% under-dispersion "probably the single most important
unresolved statistical finding". It was reasoned correctly from a stale number
in the document you audited. We re-measured on 23 August 2026, decomposing
match-total variance per character with the decomposition validated against
planted shared factors in our test suite:

| character | window | n | slope | model variance | actual variance | fitted shared sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| house (Tayler) | 2024 | 463 | 2.003 | 26.93 | 24.43 | 0 |
| house (Tayler) | 2023 | 875 | 1.589 | 26.71 | 24.06 | 0 |
| Alan | 2024 | 463 | 0.505 | 23.59 | 27.86 | 0.088 |

- The house model has **no missing shared variance**, and its total spread is
  already about 10% too wide. The original 1.644 was one configuration's
  number; slope runs 0.44 to 2.00 across characters on the same data.
- The real house fault is **discrimination**: point estimates barely vary
  between matches (sd 1.09 against outcomes at 5.29). A mean-1 shared effect
  does not move point estimates, so the Poisson-lognormal shared-intensity fix
  (§62) does not touch the defect it was aimed at.
- On §15 to 16, joint legs within a match: for the house model the measurement
  says treating legs as independent is approximately correct, which was exactly
  the practical worry. The independence caveat stays printed under combination
  tickets regardless.

What survives of the concern, and stays planned: a referee effect estimated
alongside team effects rather than as a confounded ratio, and modelling the
total first then allocating it to players. And one caveat we hold against
ourselves: expanding player predictions improves ECE while worsening log loss,
the two diagnostics currently disagree, that disagreement is entangled with the
live calibration question below, and we will re-run the variance study after
the calibration re-audit lands.

Your §64 reading is, on our measurement, exactly right: the player model is
genuinely stronger than the match model, and most remaining match-total spread
looks unpredictable. Three attempts to explain it have failed and the honest
open question is whether any of it is predictable at all.

## 3. Adopted into the next phase

### 3.1 The substitute programme (§4 to 12, 17 to 19, 46, 94 to 95, 106)

The best idea in the audit and our next phase's centrepiece. You are right
that a confirmed bench player currently collapses to something crude: a flat
50% appearance chance and an average 22 sub minutes, which is the averaging
mistake our minutes model was built to avoid, one level down.

What we will build, gated in the order studies-then-ship:

1. **Dataset.** Historical substitute appearances from our 81,327-row
   player-match file: entry minute (90 minus minutes played, accepting
   stoppage-time noise), position, opponent, referee, and game state at entry
   approximated from the half-time score for second-half entries, which covers
   most of them. Full game state at entry needs goal timings we do not hold,
   so §7's score-when-entering is partially blocked and approximated.
2. **Study.** Sub fouls per 90 by entry window, position and half-time game
   state; FoulRate_sub against FoulRate_starter pooled and per player; and the
   game state × substitute interaction. On §8 and §46 you are simply right:
   our three failed game-state attempts were all global, and a conditional
   effect can hide inside a flat aggregate. The study machinery is retained
   from those attempts, so this is cheap.
3. **Model change, only if the study finds signal.** Entry-time-conditioned
   exposure for the bench branch, a sub-specific rate adjustment, and the flat
   0.5 appearance prior refitted from data. Your §95 output sketch is close to
   where we want the product to land.

Replacement-type analysis (§10) is feasible only coarsely (formation slot of
the outgoing player), and is included in the study at that resolution.

### 3.2 Calibration (§51 to 54)

Your instinct that the correction needs a formal review is ahead of
information you did not have: our first 282 graded live claims suggest the
model is currently under-confident while the correction assumes
overconfidence, and this is recorded in the modelling log with an explicit
no-action discipline, because reversing a correction on 2 matchdays of data is
the fiddling the weekly review exists to prevent.

Adopted now, so the eventual decision is mechanical rather than reactive: a
versioned calibration table, PIT and interval coverage added to the metrics
module, calibration reported by segment (pre-lineup against post-lineup,
starters against subs, thin against rich evidence, per line), and a
pre-registered sample threshold at which the correction gets re-examined.
Isotonic and richer calibrators (§53) remain on the table with the caveat that
our top probability bucket held 64 observations at last fit, which is how one
corrects noise with more noise.

### 3.3 Parameter uncertainty for thin evidence (§55, 90 to 91)

Adopted as an experiment. A player at mean 1.2 on 3 effective matches
currently gets the same distribution width as one on 100. The cheap principled
version: mix the negative binomial over the Gamma posterior implied by the
shrinkage pseudo-counts, widening thin players only. Two prior negative
results do not cover this: our 1.2x and 1.4x global expansions both lost, but
those were unconditional, and evidence-conditional widening is a different
hypothesis. It goes through the harness like everything else.

### 3.4 Character governance (§31 to 35, 82, 84 to 85)

- **§34 to 35, adopted directly.** The house probability will be published
  beside every character number with the deviation quantified. Boldness is
  already defined as deviation from the pack; we will surface the number
  rather than imply it.
- **§31 and 82, adopted.** A locked final evaluation period the characters
  never touch, formalised rather than left implicit.
- **§32, a precision on Bdog.** His fade from consensus is a fixed constant
  (0.4), not a parameter fitted against outcomes, so there is no training loop
  for out-of-fold predictions to protect yet. The guard is recorded: if the
  fade is ever fitted, the design moves to OOF as you specify.
- **§84 to 85, agreed.** Lily's long memory is a product layer unless she
  earns otherwise on the locked period, and Tayler is explicitly documented as
  the calibration anchor, "least exciting" included.

### 3.5 Odds, bounded by what exists (§20 to 29, 66 to 72, 102, 107 to 109)

Your legal and durability cautions (§22, 68) are correct and match a decision
we recorded before the audit arrived. The uncomfortable fact bounding the
whole workstream: there is no licensed route to player-fouls prices at any
price that we can find. The Odds API's full soccer prop list carries cards,
shots and tackles but no fouls, and its props come from US bookmakers only.
No archive of UK fouls prices exists, historical or live. So §21's
distinction between current and historical odds collapses for our primary
market: neither is available.

What survives and is adopted: a manual price capture page for the lines we
track (timestamped snapshots, opening and closing where we can get them), the
US-books card-prop market wired as a labelled weak comparator, closing line
value measured on that honestly small sample, a quarterly re-survey of
licensed fouls-prop coverage, and your §109 rule that NO_MARKET_DATA stays
distinct from NO_EDGE, which is the same principle as our missing-opponent
rule and will be enforced the same way. Your §29 framing, market as benchmark
rather than enemy, is the right posture and we will adopt the wording.

Match-level closing odds we already hold (2000 onward, full from 2019/20) and
they drive a real market baseline for the match machinery, so the market
comparison layer gets exercised end to end even while player props stay
manual.

## 4. Registered for later, each with its blocker

- **§36 to 38, role, style and team-tactics features.** Right lens, blocked on
  data: take-ons, carries, PPDA and possession all died with the January 2026
  Opta termination and nothing free replaced them. Recent-position priors
  (rather than nominal) already exist. A coarse channel test from formation
  slots is registered.
- **§39 and 83, manager and regime effects.** Registered. Alan's claimed edge
  ("new manager or injury crisis") has never been tested on that subset, and
  it should be. Needs a hand-maintained manager-change CSV, roughly 15 rows a
  season, which fits the £0 constraint.
- **§42 to 43, referee expansion.** The pooled-alongside-teams estimate is the
  live item from the variance plan. Referee effects on distribution shape
  rather than mean is registered as a cheap study.
- **§47 to 49, home and away, competition, rest.** Home and away is a designed
  feature; rest and congestion are derivable from fixture dates we hold and
  are registered as testable. Competition matters little while the product
  covers one competition.
- **§18, substitution endogeneity.** The full test needs in-match foul timing
  we do not hold. The crude version, starters' minutes against their own fouls
  in the same match, uses data in hand and is registered.
- **§17, minutes as distributions within branches.** Registered, expected
  small: the mixture already carries the zero spike, which was the material
  win, and within-branch spread is the residual question.
- **§56, effectiveMatches naming.** Fair. Sigma-w is exposure, not the Kish
  effective sample size. Registered to show or rename.
- **§59 and 62, hurdle and Poisson-lognormal challengers.** Added to the
  challenger list, promoted only on out-of-sample distributional scoring.
- **§96, live modelling.** An explicit non-goal today, recorded as such. The
  architecture (timestamped facts, distributions, append-only predictions)
  would extend to it if the product ever changes.

## 5. Where we push back

- **§14 and 61 to 62, the shared match effect**: retired by measurement, as
  section 1 above. The attention was warranted; the remedy is not.
- **§63, joint probabilities as a priority**: dropped for the house model on
  the same measurement. The printed independence caveat stays and the question
  reopens after the calibration re-audit.
- **§86, match-level character models over-engineered**: partially conceded.
  They are the entertainment layer and the site treats them as such; the
  canonical match model you ask for exists and is the promotion baseline. We
  keep the five because a public bake-off of defensible temperaments is the
  product, under the governance adopted in 3.4.
- **§93, do not tune everything to the 3+ line**: no pushback, this is already
  the practice. Promotion is decided on distribution-level scoring, and the
  3+ overconfidence was found precisely because the model is interpretable end
  to end.

## Closing

Your core principle, predict the distribution first, price it second, compare
it with the market third, let the characters feel about it last, is close to
being this project's constitution already, and we adopt the sentence. The one
amendment experience forces on it: the market-comparison step is bounded by
whether the market exists in reachable form, and for player fouls it currently
does not. Calibration on every prediction, plus closing line value on a small
honest manual sample, is the version of "third" that reality permits, and we
would rather state that plainly than pretend otherwise.
